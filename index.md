### Adeline Kelly
PhD student in the [Mountain Limnology Lab](https://www.mountainlimnologylab.com/), based in [INSTAAR](https://www.colorado.edu/instaar/) and [EBIO](https://www.colorado.edu/ebio/) at CU Boulder. 

Limnology: the study of inland bodies of water

My research falls broadly into the disciplines of limnology, ecosystem ecology, and biogeochemistry. I am interested in lakes as sentinels of climate change and integrators of landscape disturbance; more specifically, my research focuses on 1) how mountain lakes integrate landscape disturbance/change such as wildfire, beetle kill, and increased weathering and 2) lake, landscape, and climate factors governing the sensitivity of lakes to disturbance (specifically wildfire). I intend to use primarily paleolimmnology and remote sensing to address these questions. 

I started my research career in my undergrad at the University of Kansas (Rock Chalk!) as an undergraduate research assistant in a soil biogeochemistry lab. I quickly made my way into limnology, and spent the remainder of my undergrad working in the NoCoast lab. There, I helped sample large reservoirs throughout Kansas to better understand conditions giving rise to harmful algal blooms and led a project focused on determining the impact of wildfire on grassland pond water quality. After graduating from KU, I moved to Boulder where I worked as the lab manager for my current lab for two years before starting my PhD.
